 Interim report, Third quarter 2026

 Ahold Delhaize reports resilient Q3 2026 results and updates 2026 guidance to reflect
 business disruption caused by recent cybersecurity incident

* Through our family of great local brands, we remain closely connected to the needs of customers and
 communities. Disciplined investments in value, quality and convenience are strengthening our
 competitive positions and enabling us to continue to gain market share. This balanced approach supports
 sustainable, long-term growth and positions us to deliver on our Growing Together strategic ambitions.
* Q3 net sales were €23.1 billion, up 1.9% at constant exchange rates and up 2.6% at actual exchange
 rates.
* Q3 comparable sales excluding gasoline increased 0.5% in the U.S. Several market-specific factors,
 including reduced SNAP benefits, the cyclospora outbreak and egg price deflation, alongside pharmacy
 pricing changes related to the Inflation Reduction Act, reduced our growth rate by approximately 2.1
 percentage points. This was partially offset by 0.2 percentage points due to weather.
* Q3 comparable sales excluding gasoline increased 2.1% in Europe. Accelerating volumes amid deflation
 in most of our European markets highlight the appeal of our brands’ value propositions and convenient
 shopping solutions.
* Ahold Delhaize's online sales increased 8.2% in Q3 at constant exchange rates and 9.0% at actual
 exchange rates. This was driven by strong growth of 14.2% at constant exchange rates in the U.S.,
 where customers continue to appreciate the convenience, assortments and personalization offered by our
 brands' online shopping experiences, supported by our strong omnichannel model.
* Q3 underlying operating margin was 4.1%, a decrease of 0.1 percentage points at constant exchange
 rates. A favorable discounting effect on insurance provisions at Ahold Delhaize Group, driven by interest
 rate developments, was offset by a modest decline in the U.S., in part from cycling prior-year non-
 recurring items.
* Q3 diluted underlying earnings per share (EPS) was €0.69, an increase of 1.4% compared to the prior
 year at constant exchange rates.
* Q3 IFRS operating income was €976 million and IFRS-diluted EPS from continuing operations was
 €0.72. IFRS results were €30 million higher than underlying results, primarily due to a pension settlement
 in the U.S.
* Q3 free cash flow was €445 million, driven by improvements in working capital, partially offset by higher
 purchases of non-current assets and new supplier payment regulations in Serbia.
* In October, a cybersecurity incident affected our business and, based on current estimations, is expected
 to reduce Q4 net sales by between €200 million and €250 million and underlying and IFRS operating
 income by between €75 million and €100 million. In addition, we expect incremental response and
 remediation costs to have a marginal impact on 2026 IFRS operating income (see Note 13: Subsequent
 events).
* For 2026, the Company now expects (on a 53-week basis): underlying operating margin approaching
 4.0% (previously around 4.0%); low-single-digit diluted underlying EPS growth at constant exchange
 rates (previously mid- to high-single-digit); and free cash flow of around €2.1 billion (previously at least
 €2.3 billion).
* Ahold Delhaize announces a €1 billion share buyback program to start at the beginning of 2027 and we
 aim to grow our dividend per share for 2026 at a mid-single-digit rate this year, based on this outlook.

Zaandam, the Netherlands, November 4, 2026 – Ahold Delhaize, an international food retail group and a
leader in both supermarkets and e-commerce, reports third quarter results today.

Comments from Frans Muller, President and CEO of Ahold Delhaize
“Our resilient third-quarter performance reflects the progress we are making on the execution of our
Growing Together strategy. As households remain focused on value, our brands’ deep understanding of
customers enables them to respond with the right combination of affordability, quality, healthy choices and
convenience. By listening closely and investing consistently in compelling customer value propositions, we
strengthen customer loyalty, supporting positive volume growth in both regions and market share gains
across our key markets.

“Q3 net sales grew 1.9% at constant rates (increased 2.6% at actual rates), with comparable sales growth
excluding gasoline of 1.2%. Strong cost discipline and steady execution of our Save for Our Customers
program supported a healthy underlying operating margin of 4.1%, enabling us to absorb cost pressures
from, among others, higher gasoline prices while continuing to invest in customer value. On an IFRS basis,
we delivered operating income of €976 million. This was higher than underlying operating income, primarily
due to a pension settlement related gain in our U.S. business.

“In the U.S., net sales increased 1.1% at constant rates (increased 2.6% at actual rates) and comparable
sales excluding gasoline grew 0.5%. Importantly, we delivered positive volume growth, reflecting the
strength of our brands. Top line growth continued to be influenced by several market-specific factors,
including reduced SNAP benefits, the cyclospora outbreak and egg price deflation, alongside pharmacy
pricing changes related to the Inflation Reduction Act. Together, these reduced our growth rate by
approximately 2.1 percentage points.

“Our U.S. brands remain focused on investing in price, expanding own-brand assortments and extending
their reach to serve more customers. For example, Hannaford’s renewed focus on everyday low prices and
simplified value messaging under a single ‘Save Now’ platform has improved price perception and
supported positive volume growth. These efforts are complemented by our omnichannel capabilities, which
give customers the convenience to shop with our brands whenever, wherever and however they choose.
Online sales grew 14.2%, marking the tenth consecutive quarter of double-digit growth, and online retention
continues to strengthen, reaching over 75% quarter-on-quarter. At the same time, we continue to invest in
expanding our physical footprint and enhancing the customer experience. Food Lion recently opened one
new store and launched omnichannel remodels at 92 stores in its Greensboro, North Carolina, market and
preparations are already underway for the next round of remodels in the Richmond and Raonoke markets.
This provides a strong runway into 2027, as the brand prepares to further accelerate growth.

“In Europe, net sales increased 3.1% at constant rates (increased 2.7% at actual rates) and comparable
sales excluding gasoline grew 2.1%. Positive comparable sales growth and accelerating volumes amid
deflation in most of our markets highlight the appeal of our brands’ value propositions and convenient
shopping solutions. Delhaize continued to perform exceptionally well in Belgium, supported by compelling
summer campaigns, strong seasonal assortments and a customer-focused offering that resonated
throughout the quarter. In Romania, our complementary portfolio of Mega Image, Profi and Shop & Go
positions us well to serve customers across different missions, locations and price points, creating a
platform to unlock future growth opportunities. And in Serbia, Maxi is winning back market share through
compelling own-brand offerings, attractive promotions and an extensive range of price favorites.

“Since the end of the quarter, in October, our business was impacted by a cybersecurity incident. As part of
our protocols to assess and mitigate cybersecurity incidents, we took some systems offline to help protect
our IT environment. To differing degrees, this mainly affected our online capabilities in the Benelux and the
U.S., as well as our Benelux supply-chain operations. Bol was not impacted. This, unfortunately, meant that
we were not always able to deliver the level of service our customers can expect from us. We apologize to
our brands' customers for the disruption and inconvenience this caused and are deeply grateful for their
trust, loyalty and understanding. As our brands welcome them back, we are showing our appreciation
through special promotions and other small gestures.

“I also want to sincerely thank our colleagues, who worked day and night to get our operations back on
track to serve customers. They did this in close, and highly appreciated, cooperation with our partners,
authorities and peers. While most of the disruption was contained within two weeks, work continues to fully
restore our normal operations. As we all recognize, cyber threats are an increasingly harsh reality of doing
business today, and moments like these remind us not only of our shared vulnerabilities, but also of the
strength of our relationships and the importance of standing together.

“While the cybersecurity incident has, understandably, demanded significant attention, it is important that
we keep it in perspective. Our response demonstrates the resilience of our business; the incident does not
define our business. Our strategic priorities remain unchanged, and our focus continues to be on executing
our strategy, serving our customers and creating sustainable long-term value. Our resilience enables us to
navigate disruption; our strategy ensures we continue to move forward.

“Across our business, technology, data and AI are becoming increasingly powerful drivers of productivity,
generating savings that we can reinvest in value, service and innovation for our brands' customers. A great
example of this is everything we do in mechanization. At Albert Heijn, automated Home Shop Centers have
improved productivity by more than 20%, while initiatives at Albert Heijn and bol have increased output
capacity by 10%. And what excites me is that we are moving beyond individual success stories. We are
scaling what works and bringing proven technologies to more parts of our business. That includes the new
Food Lion distribution center in North Carolina and the supply chain projects we are continuing to advance
across Europe, creating capacity for future growth, improving efficiency and, in the end, delivering an even
better shopping experience for our customers.

“Ultimately, that is what these investments are all about: using the scale and capabilities of our business to
make everyday life better for our our brands' customers. That goes beyond convenience and value. It is
also about helping them make healthier choices. Helping people live healthier lives is a clear opportunity:
our latest research among more than 8,000 consumers found that 92% want to eat healthier, yet only 14%
believe their diets are very healthy. Our brands are helping bridge that gap in practical and engaging ways.
Albert Heijn’s Hamster Chefs product range and Disney Diskeyz campaign inspire families and children to
eat more fruits and vegetables. Delhaize is investing in freshness, innovation, convenience and affordability.
The GIANT Company offers personalized nutrition guidance, pharmacy expertise and wellness resources
for shoppers using GLP-1 medications, helping them on their personal health journeys. Together, these
initiatives bring our healthy communities ambition to life, helping customers make choices that work for their
needs, lifestyles and budgets, while strengthening the trust and loyalty that underpin long-term growth.

“While the majority of value creation in our Growing Together strategy is driven by organic growth
opportunities, we are also actively looking to scale our portfolio through acquisitions. Here we follow a
disciplined approach and are particularly focused on in-market and adjacent market opportunities, where
we already have strong operational presence and brand recognition. We are pleased to announce our
agreement to acquire 100% of Tesco Czechia and Slovakia, subject to the satisfaction of customary closing
conditions, including regulatory clearances. Following completion, integration and store portfolio remodeling
for long-term success, the combined businesses of our local brand Albert and Tesco Czechia are expected
to become the leading grocery retail brand in Czechia by market share and Ahold Delhaize will be
established as a significant player in Slovakia, with a leading brand position. This is an excellent example of
our Growing Together strategy in action, bringing our local food retail expertise together with a clear capital
allocation policy to deliver long-term shareholder returns through multiple levers.

“With the year drawing to a close, I am proud of the resilience our brands and teams have demonstrated in
navigating dynamic market conditions while continuing to serve customers and advance our strategic
priorities. As we look ahead, we remain focused on sustaining momentum, strengthening local execution
and accelerating progress across our markets. Our brands are gearing up for the holiday season with
compelling promotions and inspiring seasonal assortments, helping customers make the most of the
moments that matter with friends and family. As the cybersecurity incident has had an adverse impact on
our fourth-quarter performance, we are also updating our outlook for the year.

“Based on current estimations, we expect that the incident will reduce net sales by between €200 million
and €250 million and underlying and IFRS operating income by between €75 million and €100 million. In
addition, we expect incremental response and remediation costs to have a marginal impact on 2026 IFRS
operating income. Reflecting these effects, we have updated our full-year outlook. We now anticipate
underlying operating margin to approach 4.0% (previously around 4.0%) and diluted underlying EPS growth
at constant exchange rates to be in the low-single-digits (previously mid- to high-single-digits). Taking into
account the impact of the cybersecurity incident and new supplier payment regulations in Serbia, we now
expect free cash flow of around €2.1 billion (previously at least €2.3 billion).

“Our commitment to the broader Growing Together targets remains unchanged. Our confidence in the long-
term strength of the business is reflected in the continuation of our €1 billion annual share buyback program
in 2027 and our expectations to grow our dividend per share for 2026 at a mid-single-digit rate this year.”

Outlook
As a result of the cybersecurity incident, we expect, based on current estimations, an adverse impact on
our fourth-quarter performance, reducing net sales by between €200 million and €250 million and
underlying and IFRS operating income by between €75 million and €100 million. In addition, incremental
response and remediation costs are expected to have a marginal impact on 2026 IFRS operating income.
Reflecting these effects, we have updated our full-year outlook. We now anticipate underlying operating
margin to approach 4.0% (previously around 4.0%) and diluted underlying EPS growth at constant
exchange rates to be in the low-single-digits (previously mid- to high-single-digits). Taking into account the
impact of the cybersecurity incident and new payment regulations in Serbia, we now expect free cash flow
of around €2.1 billion (previously at least €2.3 billion).
The following are other changes in the business that impact comparable performance for 2026 and that
have been incorporated into our Outlook:
 • The Inflation Reduction Act is impacting U.S. pharmacy sales. It is expected to have an
 approximate $450 million negative impact on reported and comparable store sales in the U.S.
 There is no material impact on underlying operating income.
 • The acquisition of Delfood closed on February 2, 2026, and is expected to add around €200 million
 in net sales to our Europe segment.
 • There will be a 53rd week in 2026, which is expected to have a positive impact of 1.5-2% on net
 sales and a positive impact of around 2-3% on underlying income from continuing operations. This
 does not significantly impact underlying operating margin.

Financial review
Q3 2026 (compared to Q3 2025)
Underlying operating income increased by €13 million to €946 million and was adjusted for the following
items, which impacted reported IFRS operating income by €30 million:
 • Impairments of €8 million (Q3 2025: €18 million)
 • (Gains) and losses on leases and the sale of assets of €(17) million (Q3 2025: €12 million)
 • Restructuring and related charges and other items of €(21) million (Q3 2025: nil)
The impairments mainly relate to operating stores. The gains on leases and the sale of assets mainly relate
to lease modifications and terminations. The restructuring and related charges and other items mainly relate
to a pension settlement in the U.S.
Income from continuing operations was €625 million, representing an increase of €41 million compared to
last year. This was driven by a €73 million increase in operating income, partially offset by higher net
financial expenses of €29 million and higher income taxes of €4 million.
Free cash flow was €445 million, representing an increase of €56 million compared to Q3 2025. This was
caused by an increase in operating cash flows of €134 million, higher net interest and dividend received of
€29 million and higher divestments of assets of €2 million, partially offset by higher purchases of non-

13. Subsequent events

Cybersecurity incident
In October 2026, Ahold Delhaize experienced a cybersecurity incident that mainly affected our online
capabilities in the Benelux and the U.S. as well as our Benelux supply-chain operations. Bol was not
impacted. We have since restored the majority of our affected operations and engaged relevant authorities
and other stakeholders as appropriate. The cybersecurity incident is expected to reduce Q4 net sales by
between €200 and €250 million and underlying and IFRS operating income by between €75 million and
€100 million. These impacts are reflected in the updated 2026 outlook and financial guidance (see Outlook).
In addition, we expect incremental response and remediation costs to have a marginal impact on 2026
IFRS operating income.

Announced acquisition of Tesco Czechia and Slovakia
On October 30, 2026, Ahold Delhaize announced it has agreed to acquire 100% of Tesco Czechia and
Slovakia. The transaction increases Ahold Delhaize's scale in the Central and Southeastern European
region and strengthens its ability to compete in rapidly evolving grocery markets. Ahold Delhaize will pay an
enterprise value of approximately €1.2 billion, corresponding to a lease-adjusted enterprise value of €1.7
billion (post IFRS 16). The acquisition will be funded with committed financing and existing resources, with
the transaction expected to close in 2027, following the satisfaction of customary closing conditions,
including regulatory clearances.
